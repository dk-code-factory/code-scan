How to read the scores

This page uses five different scores. Compare numbers only within the same score.

The first four are severity-weighted shares of a pool of issues. What changes is the pool, so the same model can score 0.15 in one place and 0.74 in another.

  • recall (By preference): share of the 217 confirmed issues found by 10 full scans of one 995KB TypeScript + Rust codebase at different efforts. Use this one to pick provider and effort.
  • share (Recommendations): share of the issues found by either of two full scans of the same code, one Codex and one Claude model.
  • quality (Known-bug benchmark, Cross-model calibration): share of the issues found by any model on a small 107–255KB sample. Claude models only, because Codex was not run on these samples.
  • score (Calibration curves): share of what one model found at any chunk size. It compares chunk sizes, not models.
  • precision (By language): a different kind of number, the share of a model's own findings that auto-triage marked real. Most triage was done by Codex, for Codex and Claude findings alike, so precision is an upper bound and says little about the difference between models.

Each figure comes from a single run. Differences under about 0.15 are within noise.

  • $/confirmed: scan price divided by the number of confirmed issues it found (a plain count, not severity-weighted).
  • agent-min/100KB: reviewer-agent time summed over all agents, not wall clock. Agents run in parallel, so a scan finishes much sooner.

Recommendations

Default for full scans: Codex gpt-6.1-sol at xhigh effort.

In three full scans next to a Claude model on the same code, it was clearly ahead in two and tied in the third, by share (severity-weighted share of the issues either model found): 0.76 vs Fable 0.43 on a 1.4MB Go driver, 0.81 vs Opus 0.29 on 439KB of a C++ framework, and 0.59 vs Fable 0.55 on a 2.7MB Rust driver (a tie, but Fable cost about seven times as much). These full scans used the CLI default effort (high) and cost $0.27–0.52/100KB. xhigh is recommended because in the effort study it reached recall 0.40 vs 0.31 at high, at $0.75 vs $0.43/100KB. Codex is not in the benchmark or calibration tables below, which compare Claude models only.

Best value among Claude models: Opus 5.5.

Expect about $0.40–1.20/100KB for a full scan at low to high effort; max costs about $5.90/100KB.1 Quality is split: Opus found all 7 known bugs in the Go benchmark sample and led the private Go calibration sample (0.74), with Sonnet close behind (0.68, within noise) and Fable at 0.43. Fable scored higher on the other two calibration samples (see below) at about twice the price on samples and five times on the one full scan where both ran (Rust driver). Use Opus for a Claude-only pipeline, or as the second opinion next to Codex.

1 Full scans with no effort recorded cost $0.38–0.73/100KB on four codebases; on the effort-study codebase medium cost $1.01, high $1.20 and max $5.89. Calibration samples cost more ($0.62–1.19), likely because small chunks carry more per-agent overhead; use full-scan prices for your bill.

Fable 5.2: strongest on small samples, most expensive at scale.

Best quality on the C++ framework sample (0.65 vs Opus 0.48) and the 255KB Go driver sample (0.49 vs Opus 0.29), though behind Opus on the 107KB known-bug sample of the same driver (0.44 vs 0.50, 6 of 7 bugs vs 7), and third of four on the private Go sample, behind Opus and Sonnet (0.43 vs Opus 0.74). At scale it is expensive: the full 2.7MB Rust driver scan cost $1.93/100KB, about seven times Codex and five times Opus on the same code. Use it for a focused scan of a critical subsystem (--only), not for a whole large repo.

Sonnet 5.5: cheap but inconsistent. Haiku 5.5: don't use for review.

Sonnet scored 0.68 on the private Go sample but 0.09 on the C++ sample, and found 4 of 7 known Go bugs. That 0.09 was at the 60KB chunk its calibration picked, a size where quality often drops (its C++ curve was not kept), so it may be partly a chunk-size effect. Haiku found 2 of 7 and scored 0.22–0.32; its C++ run failed with no result. Its reviewers often stopped before reporting on all their assigned files, so its results are incomplete.

For critical code, run two models side by side.

Different models find mostly different issues. On the Go driver, 25 issues were found by both Codex and Fable, while 74 were found only by Codex and 47 only by Fable. On the Rust driver: 12 by both, 35 only by Codex, 51 only by Fable. Fable found more there, but Codex's were more severe (22 critical or high vs Fable's 7), so its severity-weighted share is higher (0.59 vs 0.55). In the study above, Codex xhigh alone reached recall 0.40, and 0.66 together with Opus max. That pair cost $6.64/100KB in total. To do this, ask /code-scan:scan for "the default review on Codex at xhigh effort and on Opus at max effort": it registers a profile per model and runs both on the same commit and plan. The default efforts (Codex high, Opus unset) are cheaper but were not measured as a pair. Then /code-scan:compare shows what only one of them found.

By language

"Compared" means two or more models reviewed the same code. Where only one model ran, the general recommendation applies. Prices are from full scans: Codex at xhigh or the CLI default (high), Opus at medium or with no effort set (level not recorded). Two prices in one cell are separate scans of different codebases at different effort levels, not a low–high range. "not recorded" means the scan ran but its price or effort was not saved. Precision is an upper bound (see How to read the scores). "Opus chunk" is the size init picked: the largest size before quality fell more than 0.15 below the best, which is not always the best-scoring one (see Chunk size). The TypeScript row is the TypeScript + Rust codebase also counted under Rust, not a separate measurement.

Known-bug benchmark

Claude models only. Codex was not run on this sample. Haiku's reviewers often stopped before reporting on all their assigned files, so its row is incomplete. "calibration $" is the one-time cost of running init for that model. It is not paid again on each scan. Calibration picked 90KB for Fable on this sample (its curve is not shown) but 40KB on the 255KB sample of the same Go driver (the curve below), and at 20KB Fable found all 7 bugs. Sonnet likewise got 20KB here but 60KB on the 255KB sample, so its scores in the two tables are not at the same size. A calibrated size is a good starting point, not an exact optimum.

Cross-model calibration

Claude models only, each at its own calibrated chunk size. Quality is the share of the distinct issues any model found on that sample. Differences under about 0.15 are noise. The Go driver sample here (255KB) is not the known-bug benchmark sample (107KB). It was calibrated in a separate run, so chunk sizes and scores differ from the benchmark. Quality here is not comparable to the curve scores further down (same model and size can show 0.29 here and 0.75 there): each uses a different pool, see How to read the scores. The Go driver run did not record prices, so its table has no price columns, and Haiku was not run on it. "calibration $" is the one-time cost of init.

Chunk size

Let init calibrate it, and expect 20–40KB.

Review quality falls off sharply once a reviewer gets too much code at once. Across the 12 calibration curves below, the best-scoring size was 20KB or 40KB in 9. In 8, quality fell by a quarter or more one step past the best size (Fable on the C++ framework: 0.78 at 40KB, 0.39 at 60KB). init keeps growing the chunk until the score falls more than 0.15 below the best seen so far, then keeps the size before that: not always the peak. Two Opus runs picked 90KB: on the C/C++ driver API in Rust 90KB was the peak (0.86), and on the TypeScript + Rust driver it scored 0.50 against 0.64 at 40KB. That is why By language shows 90KB for Rust and TypeScript. C# took 40KB over the best-scoring 20KB because 0.78→0.67 is within 0.15. The one outlier, Sonnet on the Go database driver (0.23 at 40KB, 0.69 at 60KB, so init picked 60KB), is most likely single-run noise, not a reason to use bigger chunks. init is a one-time cost of $1–5 per model for Sonnet and Opus and $7–15 for Fable. Every later scan reuses the result.

Calibration curves

Score per chunk size within one model's calibration (share of the distinct issues that model found at any size). Compare sizes within one line, not across models. One sample per size, so differences under about 0.15 are noise. Hover a point, or open "values" under a chart, to read exact scores.
